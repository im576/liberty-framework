# Look, feel and performance plan

2026-09-25, Claude. Based on the owner's latest log, the installed data files, and prior research ([Performance.md](Performance.md), [Dismemberment.md](Dismemberment.md), [ViolentLiberty.md](ViolentLiberty.md)).

## Where the lag is now

The latest session, after direct natives:

- **Quiet areas:** frame p50 was 10 ms (about 100 FPS). The whole framework costs about 5 ms per frame: gunplay ~3.5 (setup 1.8 + weapon ~1.5), Arsenal ~1.4, holsters ~0.4, combat ~0.1–1.
- **Busy streets and fights:** p50 was 20–58 ms, with the framework cost unchanged. **The remaining lag is the game itself:** population (peds and cars are CPU-bound), rendering, and HDD streaming.
- All 18 direct natives verified (`direct_natives verified=18/18`); the game thread is parked during our ticks.
- The skipped cuts in this log (`combat_sever_skipped ... collapse_failed`) came from the `maximumSeveredPeds` cap (4) being full, not from failed bone lookups.

## Principles for building more without hurting this PC

1. **Frame budget.** The framework's target is 2 ms per frame in total. Every module reports to `CostMeter`. A module over budget is logged and shown on a DevTools performance page.
2. **Never poll through SHDN in a hot path.** Use direct natives and engine memory reads (proven safe), and event hooks where possible.
3. **Event-driven:** hook the engine's damage/impact and weapon-fire paths with the native stub technique already used for dismemberment. This replaces health scans and clip polling, and gives exact impact positions.
4. **Data, not code, for look and feel:** timecycle, weather schedule and effect lists live in data files with live reload. Tuning a look must not need a rebuild.
5. **Adaptive:** when frame time rises, cut the optional work first (ambient density, gore effect counts, cosmetic particles), then restore it. Gameplay-critical work is never cut.
6. **Every heavy asset is opt-in and measured:** textures, ReShade, AI upscales and new models each get a before/after capture.

## Performance: the remaining game-side lag

- **Density governor (planned code):** GTA IV's CPU cost scales with peds and cars. Each frame, the governor sets `SET_PED_DENSITY_MULTIPLIER` / `SET_CAR_DENSITY_MULTIPLIER` from a smoothed frame time. Example targets: full density at ≤ 25 ms, easing down to 60 % at ≥ 45 ms.
- **Stable frame cap:** a steady 40 FPS feels better than 30–90 swings. Use FusionFix `FrameLimitType = 2` with the in-game FPS limit set to a custom 40 (or 45), and test.
- **Free upscaling:** the game runs borderless windowed, so Magpie (free, open source) can apply FSR to a 1600×900 or 1280×720 render. Only worth it if captures show the GPU is the limit.
- **Free owner-side wins:** move the game to the SSD; turn Night Shadows off and set Shadows to High; turn off the Steam overlay and shader pre-cache.

## Mood: gloomier, wetter, colder, and less crushed-black

GTA IV's look comes from `update/pc/data/timecyc.dat` and `timecycext.dat` (FusionFix's versions). They hold per-weather, per-hour ambient/direct light, sky, fog, exposure, bloom, colour correction, desaturation, contrast, gamma, volumetric fog and sun shafts. FusionFix's `ExtendedTimecycEditing = 1` enables live reload (F3).

1. **"Liberty Mood" timecycle, generated by our own tool** (reproducible, tunable, with the original backed up):
   - Cooler colour correction and slight desaturation on overcast and rain.
   - Denser low fog and volumetric fog on cloudy, drizzle, rain and fog weathers.
   - Softer, flatter sun on "grey" days.
   - **Dark fix:** raise the night/interior ambient multipliers and the exposure floor (`LumMin`), and lift shadow gamma slightly. Night stays moody but readable, without crushed blacks.

   Zero runtime cost: it's the game's own grading.
2. **Weather director** (a small script with a JSON schedule):
   - A weighted daily forecast favouring overcast, drizzle, rain and fog, with occasional "cold front" runs.
   - Transitions use the game's weather natives. Days persist and don't flicker.
3. **Cold and wet details:**
   - On cold days, breath vapour puffs (stock `ped_breath` / `ped_exhale_vapour`) on the player and a few nearby peds, time-sliced.
   - Rain wetness, splashes and camera drops come from the game and FusionFix. Violent Liberty's blood already interacts with wet surfaces.

## Feel: weapons, aiming, gore

- **Weapons:** sound is the biggest missing piece of feel (shot punch, tails, reload clicks). This needs audio assets; use permissioned packs or original sounds.
- **Hit feedback:** the damage-event hook enables exact impact wounds and optional subtle hit sounds.
- **Dismemberment:** original stump caps (neck, shoulder, elbow, hip, knee) plus exact impact positions from the damage hook. Raise the cut-body cap now that upkeep is cheap.
- **Aiming:** keep the current feel (the owner likes it). Later, polish aim transitions with camera easing.

## AI and graphics

- **AI-upscaled textures** (e.g. Real-ESRGAN via the Vulkan-based `realesrgan-ncnn-vulkan`, which runs on AMD):
  - They raise VRAM and streaming load, and this PC has 4 GB VRAM, 8 GB RAM and an HDD.
  - Only after the SSD move. Only selected surfaces (roads, sidewalks, eye-level walls) at 2×, through our WTD pipeline, with measurements.
  - No blanket packs.
- **ReShade and LUTs:** possible over DXVK, but the timecycle already does grading at zero cost. Use ReShade only for effects the timecycle can't do (e.g. a light sharpen), and test with Violent Liberty.
- **RTX Remix:** not applicable (GTA IV uses programmable shaders).
- **Frame generation:** AFMF needs RDNA2 or newer. Lossless Scaling is paid. So neither for now.
