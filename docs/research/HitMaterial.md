# T-050 — hit material from the existing ray result

**October 1 qualification:** the runtime observations below used **Quick development mode**, not full acceptance.
Seven sampled correlations support a candidate 0-based material-row interpretation. Complete taxonomy,
wood/glass/water/prop coverage and effective override identity remain unverified; Stage 1 consumption is pending.

**Decision: works with sampled limits (runtime observations September 30; full acceptance pending).**

**Stage 1 use:** a hit's name is `materials.dat` row `id`. `config/impact-surfaces.json` (generated by
`tools/research/build_impact_surfaces.py`, schema in CONFIG_SCHEMA.md) holds all 156 rows with their impact class, built for the
stock file (its SHA-256 is stored in `materialsDatSha256`; check it against the installed file at startup). Lane C looks up
`rows[id].impact` and builds the effect name from it: `imp_bullet_<impact>` and `imp_shotgun_full_<impact>` for
`concrete`, `wood`, `metal`, `glass`, `default`. Counts: concrete 20, wood 18, metal 36, glass 19, default 41, ped 22.
`ped` rows (body parts, ids 99-122) are not surface impacts: gore handles them, and `imp_bullet_armour` is selected from the
ped's armour state, not from a material. `water` rows (96-98) map to `default` with `water: true`. Rows past 98 are
offline-derived (the table skips blank lines like the game's parser; ids above 99 are not yet hit in game).
**Limits:** (1) a downward ray that starts inside the player returns the player ped (`Inconclusive`, material UNKNOWN), so
start rays from a point outside the body; (2) a ray of the `objects` mask along the forward axis missed the corpbarrier prop
that `rayto prop` hit, so the **prop material is not observed** (only world, vehicle and ped are); (3) 7 rows of 156 were
sampled; the mapping rule is proven by 7 of 7 agreeing, not by a full table walk; (4) water, glass and wood rows were not
reached. The first (2026-09-29) run's raw `raydebug` dumps are consistent: the vehicle dump carries `0x4F` = 79.
The game's successful line-test consumer feeds the packed dword at **result +0x48** to its
material manager, whose adapter extracts **the low byte** as the material index.
The smallest diagnostic is `index = raw.Raw[18] & 0xFF`, alongside the unchanged 24-word
`raydebug` dump. No ABI change, hook, extra ray, bounds dereference or new game call is needed.
This is sufficient to prepare the first scenario; it does not satisfy the task's in-game acceptance.

Evidence labels in this note: **VERIFIED OFFLINE** = measured repository source, local disk-image
instructions, or local data-file contents; **PLAUSIBLE** = inference awaiting loaded-file or
surface correlation; **UNKNOWN** = not established. No new in-game evidence was collected.

## Exact result-to-material chain

**VERIFIED OFFLINE:** `0xA536B0` forwards the caller's result pointer to `0x738880` unchanged.
At `0xA53757`: `FF 75 14` pushes `[ebp+0x14]` (fourth line-test argument).
`0xA5375F: E8 1C 51 CE FF` calls the physics query. That query invokes `0x767840`
with the result as its second argument. At `0x76786F: 8B 44 24 10`, the setup reads it,
and `0x767881: 89 46 20` stores it in the query object's `+0x20`.

**VERIFIED OFFLINE:** a direct consumer in `0xD5FA40` calls this same line test:

```text
D5FADE  6A 04                     push 4
D5FAEB  6A 01                     push 1
D5FAED  68 8E 00 00 00            push 0x8E
D5FAF2  8D 84 24 AC 00 00 00      lea eax,[esp+0xAC]
D5FAF9  50                        push eax             ; result
D5FB19  6A 00                     push 0               ; ignore
D5FB1B  8D 44 24 44               lea eax,[esp+0x44]
D5FB1F  50                        push eax             ; end
D5FB20  57                        push edi             ; start
D5FB38  E8 73 3B CF FF            call 0xA536B0
D5FB3D  83 C4 1C                  add esp,0x1C
D5FB40  85 C0                     test eax,eax
D5FB42  0F 85 BB 00 00 00         jne 0xD5FC03
D5FC03  FF B4 24 A0 00 00 00      push [esp+0xA0]       ; hit instance
D5FC0A  E8 11 D8 C9 FF            call 0x9FD420
D5FC17  8B 94 24 EC 00 00 00      mov edx,[esp+0xEC]    ; result+0x48
D5FC4D  83 C4 04                  add esp,4
D5FC54  89 54 24 08               mov [esp+8],edx
D5FC7E  8B 35 68 89 8B 01         mov esi,[0x18B8968]   ; material manager
D5FC84  52                        push edx
D5FC85  8B 06                     mov eax,[esi]
D5FC87  8B CE                     mov ecx,esi
D5FC89  8B 40 14                  mov eax,[eax+0x14]
D5FC8C  FF D0                     call eax
D5FC8E  66 83 78 20 1E            cmp word [eax+0x20],0x1E
```

**VERIFIED OFFLINE stack accounting:** call the stack before the first argument push `S`.
There are three pushes before `D5FAF2`, so `lea [esp+0xAC]` computes `S+0xA0`.
After the line-test call, `add esp,0x1C` restores `S`. The instance push at `D5FC03`
lowers esp to `S-4`; the cdecl helper leaves that argument on the stack. Consequently
`[esp+0xEC]` is `S+0xE8 = result+0x48`. This is a field derivation, not a guessed offset.
The alternate miss path uses `0xA54510` and converges at `D5FC03`; the direct line-test
success branch above independently establishes the field for the existing engine query.

**VERIFIED OFFLINE:** the manager constructor at `0xCF2940` stores vtable `0xEDE9C4`.
The table's slot `+0x14` points to `0xCF2BC0`, and slot `+0x34` to `0xCF2BF0`:

```text
CF2BC0  8B 11                     mov edx,[ecx]
CF2BC2  0F B6 44 24 04            movzx eax,byte [esp+4]
CF2BC7  89 44 24 04               mov [esp+4],eax
CF2BCB  FF 62 34                  jmp [edx+0x34]
CF2BF0  0F B7 41 18               movzx eax,word [ecx+0x18] ; count
CF2BF4  56                        push esi
CF2BF5  8B 74 24 08               mov esi,[esp+8]          ; byte index
CF2BF9  3B F0                     cmp esi,eax
CF2BFB  8B 41 14                  mov eax,[ecx+0x14]       ; record base
CF2BFE  73 0A                     jae 0xCF2C0A
CF2C00  8B D6                     mov edx,esi
CF2C02  C1 E2 04                  shl edx,4
CF2C05  2B D6                     sub edx,esi
CF2C07  8D 04 90                  lea eax,[eax+edx*4]      ; base + index*60
```

**VERIFIED OFFLINE:** this means `(result_dword_48 & 0xFF)` is the lookup index;
the other 24 bits are not included in the material-table lookup. Their individual meanings
are **UNKNOWN** here. The lookup falls back to the base record when the index is out of range;
the diagnostic should report out-of-range as UNKNOWN rather than silently label DEFAULT.

## Data-file mapping

**VERIFIED OFFLINE:** the loader `0xCF2F60` uses the local string
`0xEDE6E0 = "common:/data/materials/"`, appends `.dat`, reads a `%f` version,
skips lines starting `#`, extracts a `%s` name, and calls manager vtable slot `+0x30`.
That slot points to `0xCF29E0`, which takes the current count `[manager+0x18]`,
computes `base+count*0x3C`, increments the count, and assigns the name.
At `0xCF30B1: E8 0A C5 06 00`, the loader calls parser `0xD5F5C0` on that record.
The 2.00 parser reads name/FX group/heli group followed by the numeric fields; its
`0xD5F68E: 66 89 46 20` stores the resolved FX group at record `+0x20`.
`0x6935C0: 8B 44 24 04 89 41 08 C2 04 00` assigns the name pointer at record `+8`.
Thus material IDs are **zero-based data-row positions**, excluding the version, comments
and blank lines; the second column gives the FX group. Material ID and FX group ID differ.
The trailing repeated name is editorial: the parser format has 16 conversions and ignores
column 17. For example file line 223 starts WINDSCREEN_WEAK but ends WINDSCREEN; use the
first column as the material name, never the trailing label.

**UNKNOWN:** whether an overloader/mod substitutes another materials.dat, whether every

An installed WATER row is not evidence for water ray hits. A solid corpbarrier1 establishes
that the ray hits its bounds, not that its material must be metal. Proxy collision reports
the proxy's material; it need not match the visible authored wall. Ped capsule results may
use generic PED instead of the visible body's anatomical material; dynamic/fragment and
ragdoll paths still need validation. Dismemberment's bone lookup is a separate capability.

## Reproduce without starting the game

From this worktree in PowerShell (the bundled runtime is already present):

```powershell
$py = 'C:\Users\IM576\.cache\codex-runtimes\codex-primary-runtime\dependencies\python\python.exe'
$game = 'D:\SteamLibrary\steamapps\common\Grand Theft Auto IV\GTAIV'
$objdump = 'D:\GTAIV-Reborn-Tools\toolchains\llvm-mingw-20260922-ucrt-x86_64\bin\llvm-objdump.exe'
& $py tools/research/material-probe.py audit --exe "$game\GTAIV.exe"
& $py tools/research/material-probe.py audit --exe "$game\GTAIV.exe" --objdump $objdump
& $py tools/research/material-probe.py decode --materials "$game\common\data\materials\materials.dat"
& $py tools/research/material-probe.py decode --materials "$game\common\data\materials\materials.dat" --log '<captured LibertyFramework.log>'
```

The audit checks the executable hash, PE32 section mapping, exact instruction bytes and
manager vtable slots, then reports exact-pattern occurrences in `.text`. The optional
LLVM dump includes the complete stack-accounting and loader/lookup intervals. Its forensic
pins are not ASLR-safe runtime signatures. The decoder preserves packed/tail words and
capture context, annotates only successful nonzero-instance hits, and labels name matching
PLAUSIBLE until effective-file identity is established. No commercial resources are copied
into the repository. No API/core/shared/task/state files were edited and no commit was made.
